You’re right: a single page works for a quick intro, but a care business needs more depth. Families are making a trust decision about who enters their home, and Google also ranks separate pages for each service far better than one long page.

**How to decide the structure**

1.  **List what visitors need to know** before they call: what you do, who the nurses are, how much it costs, where you work, and how to book.

3.  **Check it against the real customer.** Ask the client what families ask on the phone. Those questions become your FAQ and page content.
4.  **Keep the menu to 5-6 items** so a phone visitor is never lost.

-   **Home:** the current design, trimmed to highlights.

what about for desktop and tablet because those have unique design constrait so you cant fit a mobile template exactly to a desktop view without looking broken

## Claude responded: You're right.

You’re right. Scaling one layout up makes desktop look stretched and empty, so each device needs its own layout rules. The same content and components are reused, but the arrangement changes.

**Design each size on its own terms**

|     | Phone | Tablet | Desktop |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **How it’s used** | One thumb, scrolling | Touch, often held two-handed | Mouse, wide screen |
| **Layout** | One column | 2 columns | Split layouts, 3-4 columns |
| **Navigation** | Menu button plus sticky call bar | Full menu in the header | Full menu plus hover states |
| **Images** | Full width, stacked | Beside the text | Large, with text overlapping or beside |
| **Text width** | Edge to edge | Capped around 60 characters per line | Capped around 60-70 characters, never full width |

**Rules that stop desktop looking broken**

1.  **Cap the content width** (we use 1180px) so lines never stretch across a huge monitor.
2.  **Change the layout, not just the size.** On phone a service is a stacked card; on desktop it’s a 4-column grid; the hero goes from stacked to side by side.
3.  **Use more space deliberately.** Desktop gets larger headings, more padding, and two-column sections instead of long single lists.
4.  **Add desktop-only helpers** such as hover effects, a sticky side summary, or a mega menu for the 8 services.
5.  **Remove phone-only helpers** on larger screens, like the sticky call bar.

**How to decide each page’s layout**

-   Sketch every page at three widths: about 390px (phone), 820px (tablet) and 1280px (desktop).
-   For each section, ask “what is the best use of this width?” rather than “how do I shrink this?”
-   Test on real devices, plus an ultrawide monitor (1920px+) and a small laptop (1024px).

Our current build already does this in a basic way with two breakpoints (700px and 1040px). For the full multi-page site, I’d write a **responsive layout spec** into the design document, showing exactly how each page section changes on phone, tablet and desktop.

**Use both, with WhatsApp as the main button and a short form as the backup.** This is the hybrid option from the document.

**Why not WhatsApp only**

-   Nothing is saved, so if a message gets buried, that family is lost.
-   You can’t see how many enquiries came in, or which service or area they came from.
-   Some people, like relatives abroad or those who want something formal, won’t message a business on WhatsApp.

**Why not form only**

-   Many Ugandan families would rather chat or call than fill in a form.
-   A form feels slower, and a missed alert means a long wait for the family.

**What I’d put on the Contact page**

1.  Big **Call** and **WhatsApp** buttons at the top, for people who want an answer now.
2.  Below them, a **short form** with only: name, phone, who needs care, care needed, and area. Keep everything else optional.
3.  After sending, a thank-you message with a “Continue on WhatsApp” button, so the family can chat immediately and you still have the record.

**If the client wants it simplest for now:** launch with WhatsApp and call buttons only (what the current design already does), and add the saved form when the admin panel is built. Nothing needs redesigning later.
